# Rendering mechanisms: evidence and competing reconstructions

Research date: 2026-10-16. Research only; no implementation selected or changed.

## Evidence and provenance boundary

The brief associates these with approximately 1.54, 3.27, and 5.01 seconds of verified playback and advancing frame counters. Those timings are supplied evidence, not independently measured here. The images themselves show:

- An oblique view of a boat and bird surrounded by broad, curved, roughly concentric light/dark bands; green shadows and pale yellow highlights dominate.
- The light/dark contours near the boat and its dark surrounding region differ across the three frames. The hull remains recognizable and comparatively stable, although its apparent position/outline also changes slightly.

These observations do **not** establish physical fluid simulation, live interactivity, true geometry displacement, ray tracing, a particular dither matrix, or even a real-time renderer. Specular-looking rings can be authored masks. A prerecorded offline scene can have the same appearance as a real-time shader.

## Competing scene and deformation hypotheses

Confidence below concerns consistency with the inspected frames, not verified provenance. The cheapest explanation should remain in contention until evidence eliminates it.

| Hypothesis | What it explains; evidence against it | Discriminating evidence and smallest proposed experiment | Temporal behavior and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1: Layered image with animated UV deformation and ring lighting** (medium plausibility; strongest economical alternative to a full scene) | A boat/bird cutout over a water image or procedural background, plus a projected radial warp and light/dark masks, can preserve the central silhouette while moving surrounding bands. A separate reflected/occluded region can reproduce apparent depth. Changing hull exposure and coherent occlusion would require careful masks; genuine changing viewpoints or newly visible hull surfaces would weaken this explanation. | First freeze the reference in adjacent frames and track hull corners, bird, seams, and background features separately. Then propose a single still plus foreground mask, elliptical radial phase and UV displacement. If modest warping reproduces the observed motion without bent hull seams or missing pixels, 3D is unnecessary for this crop. Test a checkerboard source to expose displacement direction and mask boundaries. | Stateless phase animation is deterministic at any timestamp and cheap: roughly one full-screen shading pass plus texture reads. Disocclusion produces stretched/empty texels; large warps reveal flatness. Screen-space rings need perspective mapping to avoid looking like an overlay. |
| **H2: Shaded height surface plus independent rigid boat** (medium plausibility; preferred *structural reconstruction hypothesis*) | A water plane with a scalar height field, slope-derived normals, reflected light/environment and a boat mesh or card naturally separates stable hull from moving shading. Broad highlights need not correspond directly to height maxima. The frames provide no clear silhouette change of the water or demonstrable parallax, so geometry is not yet justified. | Use identical analytic height data twice: once only to perturb normals on a flat plane, once to displace a tessellated plane. Keep camera, light and boat fixed. Compare crest occlusion at the hull, apparent band thickness, and reflection deformation with dithering disabled. Only retain displacement if it supplies a visible missing cue. | An analytic wave sum has no accumulated numerical drift; aliasing depends on spatial frequency and sampling. Vertex work scales with mesh density; normal shading and reflections scale with pixels. A simple reflection texture is much cheaper than dynamic scene reflection or ray tracing. |
| **H3: Stateful 2D wave solver feeding normals/displacement** (low-to-medium plausibility) | Impulses in a height/velocity texture can create propagation, interaction and persistent disturbance. This would be useful if the reference has local impacts, reflected waves or convincing responses to a moving body. Three widely separated frames show no measured impulse, boundary reflection or causal interference that requires a solver. | Compare a single analytic impulse with a ping-pong height/velocity solver under the same shading; then add two offset impulses and an obstacle. Look specifically for boundary behavior and obstacle interaction. Merely seeing crossing rings is insufficient: analytic waves also superpose. | Needs fixed/substepped simulation time, bounded damping and reset behavior. Cost scales with simulation texels and substeps, plus normal/render passes. Dropped frames or variable integration can change dynamics; small textures risk grid anisotropy and numerical dispersion. A height solver alone does not supply foam, fluid volume, buoyancy or boat coupling. |
| **H4: Fully authored/offline 3D animation, then stylized video** (medium plausibility; strongest provenance alternative) | A rendered boat, water and compositing pipeline could explain all three images without any live shader system. Authoring geometry or ring masks can give physical-looking imagery without simulation. No evidence in a reel proves interaction or runtime performance. | Seek an author project file/breakdown; otherwise inspect denser frames for genuine disocclusion, perspective-consistent reflections and lighting changes. A tiny offline render with boat proxy, animated surface and postprocess is the minimal visual comparison; it cannot establish the author's tools. | Offline rendering permits expensive reflections and compositing. Video playback cost says nothing about reproducing it interactively in this SPA. A baked clip is temporally consistent but cannot respond freely to input. |

For H2, a candidate analytic field is `h(p,t) = Σ Aᵢ envelopeᵢ(r,t) sin(kᵢr − ωᵢt + φᵢ)`, with `r` evaluated on a world plane rather than naïve screen distance. Normals can be formed from the height gradient, `normalize((-∂h/∂x, 1, -∂h/∂z))`. This is an experiment specification, not recovered reference code. A stylized color function of phase/gradient could replace physical lighting entirely.

NVIDIA's [GPU Gems water chapter](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models) is a primary implementation explanation for separating broad geometric waves from fine normal variation, deriving surface orientation, and shading reflected environments. It supports H2's feasibility, not attribution. Its legacy timings and pass counts should not be treated as this project's performance budget.

Evan Wallace's [water implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/evanw/webgl-water/master/water.js), especially lines 47–90, provides an inspectable H3 precedent: neighboring heights affect velocity, damping attenuates it, height updates, and normals are computed separately; textures are swapped between updates. It demonstrates a concrete stateful mechanism, not this reel's implementation. Its constants are not transferable stability guarantees for a different timestep or grid.

## Shading and stylization are independent choices

The broad pale arcs could be slope-sensitive reflection/specular highlights, a painted bright crest band, or a phase-to-color function. Calling them foam is unsupported: no resolved spray, bubbles or material advection is established. Test the same field with (a) direct phase color, (b) diffuse slope lighting, and (c) a broad reflection lobe. A moving light should shift a reflection highlight relative to a height crest; a phase mask should remain locked to it. Reference camera/light motion would be needed to discriminate this observationally.

Likewise, dark material below the hull could be shadow, reflected hull, an occlusion mask, depth tint, or baked source imagery. It should not be labeled refraction from these captures. A proxy boat casting an independently toggleable shadow/reflection is a more informative experiment than immediately implementing complex water optics.

## Dither hypotheses and temporal stability

| Pipeline | Observable signature / uncertainty | Minimal proposed test; implications |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordered threshold matrix (e.g. Bayer)** | Repeating grid or crosshatch in nearly uniform tones; screen-locked lattice while tone coverage changes. The frames' regular-looking fine marks are compatible, but rescaling and video compression can invent or destroy repetition. No matrix size is verified. | Apply fixed 4×4 and 8×8 threshold arrays to the same uncompressed gradient and proxy animation at a fixed internal resolution. Compare uniform patches and enlarged nearest-neighbor crops. Independent per-pixel work suits a full-screen pass. Motion can crawl at tonal boundaries even with a fixed matrix. |
| **Fixed blue-noise threshold texture** | Less obvious periodic structure and more evenly distributed stipple. A texture can be fixed in screen or object coordinates; the latter moves with surfaces and can swim under projection. | Swap only the threshold source while keeping palette and cell size identical. Pause geometry: fixed screen-space input should produce identical frames. Cheap texture lookup, but tiling/filtering and thresholds must be controlled. |
| **Frame-varying noise / spatiotemporal noise** | Grain changes even when scene shading is stationary. Three separated compressed frames cannot establish this. Random frame noise can resemble low-sample rendering noise. | Hold the scene still and compare consecutive uncompressed frames under fixed noise, independent white noise and a designed temporal sequence. Measure changed pixels in a static patch and inspect playback flicker. Temporal blue noise is not automatically a benefit without an accumulation strategy. |
| **Error diffusion or pre-dithered textures/video** | Directional/worm-like spatial texture may suggest diffusion; a texture baked into assets can move with them. Neither appearance uniquely proves the algorithm. | Compare a short frame sequence processed independently by error diffusion with the same ordered-threshold sequence. Inspect changing edges for unstable pattern rearrangement. Straight raster error diffusion has inter-pixel dependencies, making a simple parallel fragment implementation less direct; preprocessing avoids runtime work but limits interaction. |

Tanner Helland's [algorithm explanation and implementation links](https://tannerhelland.com/2012/12/28/dithering-eleven-algorithms-source-code.html) provide primary worked examples of error diffusion and ordered matrices. Ordered thresholding avoids diffusion's forward dependencies. This is a mechanism comparison, not evidence that the artist used Bayer.

Christoph Peters' [blue-noise explanation, examples and source](https://momentsingraphics.de/BlueNoise.html) demonstrate threshold-texture dithering and why spatial noise distribution affects perceived texture. NVIDIA's [spatiotemporal blue-noise article](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/rendering-in-real-time-with-spatiotemporal-blue-noise-textures-part-1/) addresses temporal sampling structure. Neither establishes that this reference uses blue noise or temporal accumulation.

A defensible trial order is: render continuous-tone shading → choose working resolution → map luminance/color toward a small palette with a threshold source → nearest-neighbor upscale if desired → overlay typography as appropriate. Compare dithering before versus after UV warping: before can bend/stretch the lattice; after keeps it screen-locked. Palette mapping and color space must be held constant between comparisons. The screenshots alone do not reveal palette cardinality, original resolution, transfer function or pipeline order.

## Next discriminating evidence and controlled trials

1. Obtain adjacent *presented* frames and native dimensions through the reference-forensics worker, excluding UI regions. Track silhouette landmarks and examine stationary tonal patches. A three-frame interval of ~1.7 seconds is too coarse for temporal-noise correlation or propagation-speed estimation.
2. Separate spatial questions: are fine marks screen-locked; does the boat reveal new surfaces; do ring spacing and ellipse compression change with depth; is dark hull-adjacent structure attached to boat or water? Record ambiguity caused by decoding, resampling and compression.
3. Propose, do not yet build, a common proxy scene and 6–10 second sequence: H1 masked image warp; H2 normal-only plane; H2 displaced plane; H3 fixed-step solver. Lock camera, colors, impulse schedule and output resolution. Compare undithered output first.
4. On the simplest plausible scene, compare ordered/fixed blue-noise/frame-varying thresholds. Evaluate both still crops and continuous playback. Record GPU frame-time distribution, peak texture memory and output under reduced internal resolution on an agreed target device; no performance promise is possible from source reasoning alone.

**Conclusion:** medium confidence in a layered explanation (scene/image content + evolving field/shading + stylization), low confidence in any specific rendering technology or dither algorithm. H2 is a useful structural reconstruction candidate; H1 is its strongest cheaper visual alternative, and H4 remains fully plausible provenance. The decisive next step is denser, native-scale temporal evidence—not a final engine or design selection.
